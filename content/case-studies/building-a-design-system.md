## The Problem

Three teams had been building the same product independently for two years. The result was three different button styles, four different ways to show an error, and no single source of truth for spacing or color.

## Approach

Rather than auditing and standardizing everything at once, I picked the five components that appeared on every screen and locked those down first. Getting buy-in on a shared button and input was enough to demonstrate value — after that, teams started requesting components rather than resisting them.

## What I Learned

A design system is more of a social project than a technical one. The tokens and components matter, but what really makes it work is giving teams a low-friction way to contribute back — and making the system easier to use than not using it.
